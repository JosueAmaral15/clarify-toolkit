Transgenics
CONCEPT: These are genetically modified foods with the alteration of the genetic code, that is, genes from another are inserted into the organism. This procedure can even be done between organisms of different species (insertion of a virus gene into a plant, for example). The procedure can be performed with plants, animals and microorganisms.

RISKS TO AGRICULTURE: transgenic species are protected by patents, which means that the farmer who decides to use them (if authorized in Brazil) will have to pay royalties to the company that owns the technology. The most immediate consequence will be the increase in farmers' dependence on transnational companies in the sector. This is because, by contractual rule, the farmer cannot use seeds from the previous planting, so he will have to buy transgenic seeds each harvest. Furthermore, it is very difficult for the farmer to completely “get rid” of transgenic plants, which can happen with any plantation, since, if he no longer wants to plant them, the chance of a transgenic plant still being born in the conventional plantation exists. If this occurs, he may be forced to pay a fine and more royalties.
In addition, there is a risk of contamination. Contamination can occur through insects or even through wind. This is the case with corn. Therefore, if there is no adequate spacing between transgenic and conventional crops, contamination can occur, taking the farmer by surprise at the time of sale. These farmers often lose contracts, as the buyer was interested in a non-GMO product. 
HEALTH RISKS: the potential risks are several and serious, and scientists have identified them as the main ones:
1. Increase in allergies When a gene from one organism is inserted into another, new compounds can be formed in that organism, such as proteins and amino acids. If this genetically modified organism is a food, its consumption could cause allergies in significant portions of the population, due to these new substances. For example, at the Institute of Nutrition in York, England, in 1999, a survey found a 50% increase in allergies to soy products, stating that the result could be attributed to the consumption of genetically modified soy. Another concern is that if the gene from a species that causes allergies in some people is used to create a transgenic product, this new product could also cause allergies, because there is a transfer of the characteristics of that species. This is what happened in the United States: reactions in allergic people prevented the commercialization of soybeans that had the Brazil nut gene (which is a famous allergen). 2. Increased resistance to antibiotics To make sure the genetic modification "worked," scientists insert genes (called markers) of antibiotic-resistant bacteria. This can lead to increased antibiotic resistance in humans who eat these foods. In other words, it can reduce or nullify the effectiveness of antibiotic-based medicines, which is a serious threat to public health. 3. Increase in toxic substances There are plants and microbes that contain toxic substances to defend themselves against their natural enemies, insects, for example. Most of the time, they do not harm humans. However, if a gene from one of these plants or microbes is inserted into a food, it is possible for the level of these toxins to increase greatly, causing harm to people, beneficial insects and other animals. This has already been seen with transgenic Bt corn, which can kill caterpillars of a species of butterfly, the monarch butterfly, which is a pollinator. Not even the toxicity of substances intentionally inserted into plants has been adequately assessed. These substances are entering food with far less safety assessment than any additive, colorant, pesticide or medicine. 4. Greater amount of pesticide residues With the insertion of pesticide resistance genes into certain transgenic products, pests and weeds will be able to develop the same resistance, becoming "super-pests" and "super-weeds". For example, Roundup Ready soybeans are resistant to the application of Roundup herbicide (glyphosate). Consequently, there will be a need to apply greater quantities of poison to crops, which represents a greater amount of toxic residues in the food we consume. In Brazil, Anvisa (National Health Surveillance Agency) authorized in 2004 a fifty-fold increase in the limit of glyphosate allowed in soy-based foods. The damage to the environment will also be serious: greater pollution of rivers and soil and incalculable imbalances in ecosystems.
ENVIRONMENTAL RISKS: the dangers that GMOs can pose to the environment are many.
The insertion of pesticide resistance genes into certain transgenic products causes pests and weeds (natural enemies) to develop the same resistance, becoming "super-pests" and "super-weeds". For example, Roundup Ready soybeans are resistant to the application of Roundup herbicide (glyphosate). This will require the application of greater quantities of poison to crops, with greater pollution of rivers and soil. There will also be imbalances in ecosystems due to the greater resistance developed over the years by pests and weeds. For Brazil, which has a unique biodiversity, the losses resulting from genetic pollution and loss of biodiversity are other serious problems related to GMOs.
